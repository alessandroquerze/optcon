Discrete-time control system and notation (Refresh)
We consider nonlinear, discrete-time systems described by
xt+1 = ft(xt, ut) t ∈N0 (1)
where xt ∈Rn and ut ∈Rm are the state and the input of the system at time t.
Notation
We use x ∈RnT and u ∈RmT to denote, respectively, the stack of the states xt for all t ∈1, . . . , T
and the inputs ut for all t ∈0, . . . , T −1, that is
x := col(x1, . . . , xT )
u := col(u0, . . . , uT −1).
where col(x1, . . . , xT ) is the (column) vector with components x1, . . . , xT .

===== PDF PAGE 3 =====
System trajectories (Refresh)
We recall the definition of trajectory of a (possibly) nonlinear, discrete-time control system.
Definition A pair (¯x, ¯u) ∈RnT × RmT is called a trajectory of system (1) if ¯xt+1 = ft(¯xt, ¯ut) for all
t ∈0, . . . , T −1. In particular, ¯x is the state trajectory, while ¯u is the input trajectory. □
Remark It will be sometimes useful to refer to a generic pair (α, µ) ∈RnT ×RmT with α := col(α1, . . . , αT )
and µ := col(µ0, . . . , µT −1) as a state-input curve. Notice that a curve (α, µ) is not necessarily a tra-
jectory, i.e., it does not necessarily satisfy the dynamics (1). □
