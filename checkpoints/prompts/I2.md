### PDF p. 19
Discrete-time optimal control: main ingredients (I)
The main ingredients of a discrete-time optimal control problem are
Dynamics: a (discrete-time) system in state space form
xt+1 = ft(xt, ut) t = 0, . . . , T −1
We will mainly work on a finite horizon, i.e. 0 < T < +∞(notation: 0, . . . , T −1). In some cases we
will also consider an infinite horizon, i.e., T = +∞.
The dynamics introduces T equality constraints
x1 = f(x0, u0) i.e. ft(x0, u0) −x1 = 0
x2 = f(x1, u1) i.e. ft(x1, u1) −x2 = 0
... ...
xT = f(xT −1, uT −1) i.e. ft(xT −1, uT −1) −xT = 0

### PDF p. 24
Trajectory generation
Consider the nonlinear, discrete-time system
xt+1 = ft(xt, ut) t ∈N0
and let
yt = ht(xt, ut) t ∈N0
be a performance output of the system.
We would like the (performance) output to follow a desired time law ydest , for t ∈N0.
Optimal Control Task
Find a (state-input) trajectory (x, u)
i.e., a function of time (xt, ut), t ∈N0, satisfying xt+1 = ft(xt, ut), for all t ∈N0, such that
yt ≈ydest for all t ∈N0.

### PDF p. 28
Example: trajectory generation for an autonomous drone (II)
Trajectory generation task: compute a (state-input) trajectory of the drone such that
• the time-evolution of the center of mass is close to a desired one, (pdesx,t, pdesy,t) for all t = 1, . . . , T
• the velocity is close to the corresponding desired velocity profile (vdesx,t, vdesy,t) for all t = 1, . . . , T
• angle and angular velocity, θt, ωt, are “not too high”
• inputs u1,t, u2,t are “not too high”.
