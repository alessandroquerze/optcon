### PDF p. 9
Discrete-time control systems and notation
We consider nonlinear, discrete-time systems described by
x(t + 1) = f(x(t), u(t), t), x(0) = x0, t ∈N0
but from now on we will use the (more compact) notation
xt+1 = ft(xt, ut), x0 = x0, t ∈N0
where xt ∈Rn and ut ∈Rm are the state and the input of the system at time t.

### PDF p. 10
Discrete-time control systems and notation
Consider a nonlinear, discrete-time system on a finite time-horizon
xt+1 = ft(xt, ut), x0 = x0, t = 0, . . . , T −1 (1)
where xt ∈Rn and ut ∈Rm are the state and the input of the system at time t.
Notation
We use x ∈RnT and u ∈RmT to denote, respectively, the stack of the states xt for all t = 1, . . . , T
and the inputs ut for all t = 0, . . . , T −1, that is
x := col(x1, . . . , xT )
u := col(u0, . . . , uT −1).
where col(x1, . . . , xT ) is the vector with components x1, . . . , xT .

### PDF p. 11
System trajectories
Let us introduce the definition of trajectory of a (possibly) nonlinear, discrete-time control system.
Definition A pair (¯x, ¯u) ∈RnT × RmT is called a trajectory of system (1) if ¯xt+1 = ft(¯xt, ¯ut) for all
t = 0, . . . , T −1. In particular, ¯x is the state trajectory, while ¯u is the input trajectory. □
Let us recall a special class of trajectories characterized by constant input and constant state.
Definition A state-input pair (xe, ue) ∈Rn×Rm is called an equilibrium pair of (1) if (xt, ut) = (xe, ue),
t ∈N0, is a trajectory of the system. □
Equilibria of discerete-time, time-invariant systems satisfy xe = f(xe, ue).

### PDF p. 12
Linearization of a system about a trajectory
Given the dynamics
xt+1 = ft(xt, ut) t ∈N0,
and a trajectory (¯x, ¯u) (i.e., ¯xt+1 = ft(¯xt, ¯ut) for all t ∈N0), the linearization of (1) about (¯x, ¯u) is
given by the linear (possibly) time-varying system
∆xt+1 = At∆xt + Bt∆ut t ∈N0,
with At and Bt the Jacobians of ft, with respect to state and input respectively, evaluated at (¯x, ¯u).

### PDF p. 14
Optimization: main ingredients
The main ingredients of an optimization problem are
Decision vector: z ∈Rd
Cost function: ℓ(z) : Rd →R cost associated to decision z
Constraints (constraint sets): for some given functions hi : Rn →R, i = 1, . . . , m, and gj : Rn →R,
the decision vector z ∈Rd needs to satisfy
hi(z) = 0 i = 1, . . . , m
gj(z) ≤0 j = 1, . . . , r.
Equivalently we can say that we require z ∈Z with
Z = {z ∈Rn | h(z) = 0, g(z) ≤0},
where we compactly denoted h(z) = col(h1(z), . . . , hm(z)) and g(z) = col(g1(z), . . . , gr(z)).
